# Tanker model and interpretation

- A single liquid free surface is approximated using weakly compressible SPH. Gas pressure is atmospheric; air compression, entrainment, fuel combustion and cavitation are absent.
- Native SPH/Chrono couples liquid forces and a free rigid-body pose in both directions. A nonrotating, accelerating support-frame surrogate applies the same inertial acceleration to liquid and dry vehicle. This is not a solved steering trajectory.
- The six wheels are rigid fixed cylinders. Road contact includes friction, but there is no suspension, wheel spin, tire force/slip curve, ABS, structural damage or cargo leak. Zero tilt while all wheels remain supported is a consequence of that rigid contact idealization.
- Dry mass is 9,000 kg; dry CG is `(0.8,0,-0.9)` m in the tank reference, 1.2 m above the road. Dry principal inertias are `(7290,43560,47610)` kg m². Water mass is 8,819.968 kg. These are declared generic inputs, not calibrated manufacturer data.
- Baffle displacement is compensated by initial water level to keep water mass identical. Dry inertia/mass remain identical; real extra plate mass is not separately represented. Four-layer 160 mm plates ensure compartment integrity at 40 mm particle spacing, not realistic wall stress.
- Transverse sealed plates limit fore/aft transfer. The additional central longitudinal plate restricts left/right exchange. The B-to-D comparison adds this longitudinal partition; the observed benefit is conditional on this combined braking/lateral maneuver.
- Accepted floating boundaries use **DBC**, not IBM or mDBC2. Thin sealed DBC pilots leaked; a fixed mDBC2 test passed but did not transfer to the moving body. The accepted thicker DBC geometry passed particle-ID isolation checks. These checks do not establish spatial convergence.
- The first side-lift indicator requires all wheels on one side to exceed 20 mm geometric clearance. It is not a reconstruction of individual tire forces. Rollover requires body-up tilt at least 75° at five consecutive saved states, roughly 0.2 s. First crossing is reported as onset.
- ComputeForces returns per-boundary-particle fluid force. Groups are disjoint: tank wall, rear/front transverse baffles, longitudinal baffle. Intersection cells are assigned to transverse plates. The sums are checked against native body loads. Local N/particle contours are not pressure or mesh-independent stress.
- All moments use instantaneous dry-vehicle CG. On-screen arrows/numbers show vector magnitudes; the final bar chart uses the body-x roll component. Displayed integrated loads use a 0.4 s filter; local colors use saved instantaneous values. Post-roll impacts are illustrative, not validated slam peaks.
- A shared 1.5–3.5 s window is used for the 32.9% peak-roll-moment reduction. Do not rank small differences in onset time as established performance without resolution and vehicle-model sensitivity checks.
- The final opening is a 2× replay. Road dashes use prescribed forward speed, while body motion comes from the solver; the cue is not total solved sliding velocity. No rollover was keyframed in the accepted final film.

Compact reference files and final videos are kept public. Full BI4/VTK particle fields and surface caches are large, reproducible working files and may be removed locally after verified publication. Re-rendering afterward requires recalculation.
