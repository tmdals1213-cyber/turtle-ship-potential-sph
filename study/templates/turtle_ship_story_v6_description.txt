Can we predict a turtle ship in waves?

Two independent calculations: a linear potential-flow model (Capytaine) and a water-only SPH model (DualSPHysics). This is an educational model demonstration, not validated historical ship performance or a controlled solver-accuracy benchmark.

The potential solution uses the mean wetted hull and a fixed reference forward speed. Its movie is ship-fixed. The waterline display mask follows the visual pose, but the hydrodynamic panels are not regenerated every frame. The large pitch response makes this a qualitative result.

The SPH ship starts at +0.5 m/s in the laboratory x direction, with all six rigid-body degrees of freedom free. There is no propulsion. Waves travel toward it in the opposite direction. The portrait result uses a lower fixed main camera, a fixed start marker, and a synchronized fixed side view. Both views use the identical measured rigid pose and reconstructed particle surface. The near-side waterline is drawn from the same SPH occupancy. Water is partly transparent in the side view for visibility. No artificial forward motion was added. All distances in that scene are model-scale.

Water can contact the initially dry part of the closed simplified hull. The SPH explanation shows the complete floating boundary, including 7,412 particles above the initial waterline out of 10,222 floating particles. Air flow, wind loading, trapped air, flooding, and hydrodynamic forces on decorative oars/masts/rigging are excluded. Complete submergence or deck-impact accuracy has not been validated. The visible water volume is a fixed crop of the larger computational tank; the free surface is reconstructed and smoothed from saved SPH particles for display.

Measured computation: potential response 153.85 s on Ryzen 9 9950X; SPH 688.30 s on RTX 5080 for 16 simulated model seconds. Different workloads and hardware; these are not speedup ratios.
